# Message house: Cohere

Built 2026-07-21 from the repo's own Cohere ICP (`icp-research/examples/cohere/icp.md`) and voice
(`brand-voice-guide/examples/cohere/brand-voice/`).

> **Real company, public data, illustrative.** A demonstration of the skill, not an official
> Cohere document. The bet, pillars, and cells are built from cited public evidence and written
> against Cohere's extracted voice standard.

## The bet

**Enterprise AI will be won at the security gate, not the benchmark.** The buyers with the largest
budgets cannot send their data out, so the category that matters is deployable AI, and Cohere
should own it rather than compete in the frontier race.

Grounding: the ICP's structural veto is security and compliance, and its primary trigger is a
GenAI mandate colliding with a hard data constraint; buyer frameworks say frontier quality
clusters while deployment posture decides regulated purchases; and the market's honest read is
that Cohere wins as the specialist, which makes the gate the right battlefield.

**Falsifier:** if hyperscaler residency guarantees prove good enough at security review for most
regulated buyers, private deployment becomes a feature, not a category. **Metric:** win rate
against hyperscaler-hosted options in deals that reached security review.

## The pillars

1. **Private by architecture.** Your data. Your infrastructure. AI that runs where your data
   lives, so security clears it. *(North on-prem/VPC/air-gapped; no training on customer data.)*
2. **Built for your knowledge.** Retrieval-strong on your own data, multilingual by default: the
   model layer for what your enterprise already knows. *(Command, Embed, Rerank as a retrieval
   stack; analyst-recognized RAG strength.)*
3. **Sovereign by partnership.** In-country AI on sovereign infrastructure, with the partnerships
   to prove it. *(Government of Canada, Bell AI Fabric, SAP, Aleph Alpha.)*

## The matrix

Each cell is the same pillar message translated for a function's moment and its committee
audience. Full cells in `house.json`; the shape:

| | Marketing (awareness) | Sales (evaluation) | Product (activation) | CS (expansion) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Private** | "The AI you are actually allowed to deploy" (champion) | "Run the security review first, not last" (CISO, economic buyer) | Deployment is the product: reference architectures (platform lead) | Expansion inherits the guarantee: a permissions conversation, not a new risk review |
| **Knowledge** | "Your enterprise already knows the answers" (champion) | "Benchmark it on your corpus, not a leaderboard" (developers) | The retrieval stack, not a chat demo (developers) | Each connected source compounds the last |
| **Sovereign** | "Sovereign AI is not a talking point here; it is deployed" (public-sector execs) | "A region promise is not an architecture" (CIO/CDO) | Sovereign partner infrastructure as a first-class target (platform lead) | Every audit passed is proof the next agency can cite |

## The spine

Consistency comes from every function standing on the same three messages; specificity comes from
translation, not from four teams inventing four stories. The shared buying moment every function
serves is the security review: marketing promises it can be passed, sales runs it early, product
keeps it true, and customer success turns each passed review into the next one's proof.

## Pressure-test

- **Weakest pillar:** sovereign-by-partnership rests on a few flagship deals; if they stay
  exceptional rather than repeatable, it is a case study, not a message.
- **The falsifier is live:** track security-review outcomes against Azure OpenAI and Bedrock
  quarterly; if their residency promises clear reviews at high rates, reposition private
  deployment as the wedge inside a multi-vendor frame.
- **The specialist honesty holds:** message the strong second vendor in a router pattern, never
  the rip-and-replace primary, and test gate-led against capability-led messaging in regulated
  campaigns.
