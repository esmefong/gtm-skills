**Signal:** Sovereign AI Factory expansion, Kamloops plus two Vancouver sites, 60,000+ GPUs by 2032
(rcrwireless, 2026-05-12).
**Trigger mapping:** the expansion is new public sovereign AI capacity, and sovereign capacity

## Touch 1: email, day 0

**Subject:** Models for the factory

Your AI Factory expansion is public: Kamloops, two new Vancouver sites, a target of 60,000 GPUs by
2032. The open question for every sovereign factory is the model layer. What runs on the racks,
privately, for customers who cannot send data out?

That is the layer we build. North and the Command family already run on sovereign Canadian
infrastructure today: fully private, nothing leaves the environment. Your factory. Your customers'
data. Our models, running inside both.

Worth a 20-minute call on what a model-layer partnership could look like for the expansion sites?

> Provenance: signal (rcrwireless 2026-05-12) · ICP trigger (sovereignty, supply side) · voice
> (possessive-contrast pillar: "Your factory. Your customers' data. Our models.") · judgment (the
> Bell AI Fabric precedent is deliberately unnamed: rival channel conflict; "sovereign Canadian
> infrastructure" carries the proof without the provocation)

The Kamloops and Vancouver expansion made the model-layer question timely. We supply private,

## Touch 3: email, day 9

**Subject:** What fills sovereign capacity

One thought since my first note. GPU capacity sells to customers who already know what they will
run on it. The regulated buyers filling sovereign capacity, banks, government, and health among
them, need models they are allowed to deploy: private, in-country, strong at retrieval on their
own data.

Offer that layer with the racks and capacity becomes an AI service. That is the partnership we
want to explore. Open to a short call with your enterprise product team?

> Provenance: ICP trigger (the regulated-buyer situation, used as TELUS's sell-through story) ·
> evidence (account-sourcing: the Rimouski factory "sold out" per TELUS's own 2025-09 release,
> demand-side pull) · voice (plain-over-jargon pillar; one commercial idea, no feature list)

## Judgment calls

- **Bell is never named.** The strongest proof available is the Bell AI Fabric deal, and it is
  deliberately withheld: naming a direct rival's partnership in a first touch reads as provocation.
  "Sovereign Canadian infrastructure" carries the claim; the precedent surfaces on the call.
- **No touch goes to procurement, security, or the CISO.** Committee rule: gates are won with
  proof through the champion, not cold sequences.
- **The internal precondition from account-sourcing stands:** brief the team on the Bell channel
  conflict before touch 1 is ever sent.
- **What would change the sequence:** a TELUS managed-AI product announcement would move this from
  partner pitch to co-sell timing; a public model-vendor commitment for the factory would send it
  back through the gate, likely to a no-send.
